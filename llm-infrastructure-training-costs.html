What does it take to train a large language model?

A visual tour from raw data to deployed assistant—and the data, compute, energy, time, and engineering effort behind each step.

Read the numbers carefully: Frontier-model training budgets are usually confidential. This graphic separates confirmed public disclosures from information that companies have not released; it does not present speculative dollar estimates as facts.

The training pipeline

A simplified lifecycle. In practice, teams repeat evaluation and tuning, and some steps overlap across large infrastructure teams.

01 · SOURCE

Collect & license data

Gather text and, for multimodal models, image, audio, or video data. Document permissions, provenance, privacy, and representation.

Data accessLegal review
02 · PREPARE

Clean & tokenize

Filter low-quality or unsafe material, remove duplicates, balance sources, and convert content into tokens the model can process.

StorageData pipelines
03 · PRETRAIN

Learn language patterns

Train a neural network to predict the next token across repeated batches. Track loss, failures, checkpoints, and hardware utilization.

AcceleratorsNetworking
04 · ALIGN

Fine-tune & evaluate

Use curated examples and preference or safety methods to improve instruction following. Test quality, robustness, bias, and safety.

Human expertiseEvaluation
05 · RELEASE

Package & deploy

Optimize serving, put safeguards and monitoring in place, and deliver through an application, API, or downloadable model.

Serving GPUsSecurity
06 · OPERATE

Monitor & improve

Measure latency, reliability, safety signals, and user needs. Retraining or updates require fresh evaluation and ongoing infrastructure.

OperationsFeedback loops
THROUGHOUT

Coordinate the system

Budget for uncertainty

Runs can be delayed by hardware faults, experiments, data changes, or failed attempts. A single “GPU-hours” figure is not the whole cost.

Four resource budgets

The bars indicate categories of effort, not a model-by-model measured share of total spending.

01 · Data

Quality, rights, and coverage

Acquisition or licensing, cleaning, labeling, deduplication, storage, and governance. Poorly sourced or unbalanced data can undermine performance and trust.

Cost varies with data source, licensing terms, modality, and preparation effort.

02 · Compute

Accelerators × time × utilization

Accelerator clusters, memory, networking, storage, software, and engineering. Longer runs and more experiments require more capacity.

GPU-hours alone omit cluster purchase or rental, networking, retries, and staff.

03 · Energy & facilities

Power for IT + cooling

Electricity use depends on hardware power, run duration, utilization, and data-center efficiency. Grid mix and location shape emissions and price.

A defensible estimate needs measured power and facility overhead—not just a parameter count.

04 · Time & people

Calendar time + expert work

Training can occupy large clusters for extended periods, but the end-to-end schedule also includes research, data work, evaluation, and deployment.

Team size, iteration, hardware availability, and reliability determine the actual schedule.

What public disclosures tell us

These examples show why careful sourcing matters: some training inputs are public, while precise infrastructure and financial costs often are not.

Meta Llama 3

Publicly reported: Meta described pretraining on over 15 trillion tokens and released 8B and 70B parameter models.

Not publicly disclosed in the cited sources: a complete training bill or definitive accelerator-hour / FLOP total.

Do not infer an official dollar cost from model size or token count.

OpenAI GPT‑4

Publicly reported: the GPT‑4 Technical Report deliberately omits model size, hardware, training compute, and dataset construction.

Not publicly disclosed in the cited source: verified training energy, duration, or total cost.

Third-party estimates are not equivalent to company-confirmed figures.

Explanatory document

Main point: training is a complete data-to-deployment lifecycle, not only a GPU run. Data quality, computing infrastructure, energy, time, and expert labor all affect feasibility and model outcomes.

Key consideration: companies disclose different levels of detail. The model examples distinguish known facts from missing information and avoid treating outside estimates as official costs.

Design rationale: a numbered pipeline communicates sequence, while four color-coded resource panels make cost drivers easy to scan. A comparison table supports direct model-to-model reading. Responsive cards, explicit labels, strong contrast, keyboard focus, reduced-motion support, and printable styles improve accessibility and use across devices.

Skill demonstrated: research synthesis, technical communication, data-literacy, responsible AI, and accessible web visualization.
